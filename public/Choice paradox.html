The Scale of the Problem

India's adoption system has a paradox at its heart

60 million children in destitution. 35,000 families waiting. Only 3,124 adoptions completed in 2021. The numbers reveal a broken system but the data alone does not explain why. This research asks: what human choices sustain this gap?

Methodology

Six weeks. Five tracks. Zero assumptions.

A rolling research design where each phase informed the next: secondary data built the tools, tools built the interviews, interviews built the insights.

Weeks 02-04

Weeks 02-04

Development and piloting of DBT, MYOS mapping, and Game It On! Three completely new instruments built for this domain.

Weeks 04-06

DBT and MYOS with 5 APs and 2 PAPs. HSR counsellor interviews. Families of Joy panel. Game It On! with 30 public participants.

Data analysis, affinity mapping, synthesis. Culminating in the Choice Paradox framework and policy recommendation report.

The Research Tools

Three instruments built from scratch

Standard interviews reveal what people did. To access what they believe subconsciously, and how society perceives adoption at large, we designed three tools each targeting a completely different layer of the research question.

Tool 01 · Applied with 5 APs and 2 PAPs

DBT
Did.Believe.Think

The DBT tool was designed to tap into the subconscious of the interviewee. Parents were open to sharing what they did. But to understand what they truly feel, we needed to reach what they believe and think.

By placing participants in an advisory position, asking them to react to anonymised stories rather than narrate their own, DBT creates triangulated access to subconscious motivations that direct questioning never surfaces.

Why it works: When people stop narrating and start reacting, the truth emerges. The advisory frame dissolves defensiveness and activates genuine belief, not performed acceptance.

Tool 02 · Participatory Mapping

MYOS
Map Your Own Story

MYOS invites participants to spatially map their emotional journey, key milestones, support networks, and identity relationships across the adoption timeline simultaneously for both parent and child.

Externalising experience onto a physical map reduces the emotional burden of narrating difficult or stigmatised experiences directly. Families became co-authors of a map, not subjects of an interview.

Spatial externalisation lowers the emotional cost of narrating stigmatised or painful experiences

Tool 03 · 30 Public Participants

Game It On!

Understanding the general perception of such a taboo topic required a completely different approach. We designed an interactive game placing participants in the role of a couple navigating life decisions around having a baby and adoption.

By framing decisions as game choices rather than personal beliefs, Game It On! surfaced unconscious bias that direct questioning would suppress. Participants made dramatically different decisions for the fictional couple than they would claim to make for themselves.

The key finding: Society does not yet give parents true social permission to adopt openly. This silent discouragement from communities and extended family acts as a powerful inhibitor long before families even register.

Key Insights

What the subconscious finally revealed

The three tools together produced a layered picture of the Choice Paradox: the interplay of subconscious beliefs, social permission, and systemic friction.

Insight 01 · Motivation

The desire to parent is not the same as openness to adoption

PAPs strongly want to parent, but their picture of parenthood is shaped by biological norms. Adoption arrives only after all other paths close, making it a last resort, not a first choice. This framing amplifies expectations and shapes the entire post-placement experience.

"We tried everything else first. Adoption was always in the back of our minds but not as the plan. As the backup."

DBT participant, AP of 4 years
01
Insight 02 · Preference Filter

Age, gender and health preferences create a near-impossible bottleneck

Most PAPs want a healthy child under age 2. Only 61 such children existed in India in 2021 against 35,000 families waiting. The expectation gap, not a shortage of children, is the real crisis. DBT revealed these preferences as subconscious, not consciously reasoned.

02
Insight 03 · Social Permission

Society does not give parents permission to adopt openly

Game It On! revealed a deep gap between stated acceptance and lived social reality. Silent discouragement from extended family, communities, and institutions acts as a powerful inhibitor long before families even register with CARA.

03
Insight 04 · Post-Placement

The system disappears at the moment families need it most

Post-adoption support drops to near-zero after formal placement. The 5% who return their child are evidence of a system that never prepared them. Critical needs emerge in years 2 and 3, when all institutional support has already ceased.

04
5%
Dissolution rate

Adoption dissolution: a designable problem

5% of adoptive placements break down in India. This does not reflect failed love. It reflects failed preparation, misaligned expectations, and a support vacuum. Every breakdown is a failure of the system and every failure of the system is a design opportunity. MYOS data showed that parents with the richest understanding of what to expect reported the highest satisfaction and the lowest rates of crisis, proving the causal link between preparation quality and placement outcome.

"Nobody told us there would be a year where she tested every boundary every single day. Nobody prepared us for that and nobody asked her what she needed either."
Adoptive father, 3 years post-placement · MYOS research session

Address stigma upstream

Design-led campaigns targeting ambient social stigma revealed by Game It On!, building social permission for adoption before families even begin the process.

Counsel expectations before registration

Mandatory expectation-setting at PAP registration to address the age, gender, and health filter before it becomes a years-long bottleneck.

Design the waiting period as a service

The 2 to 7 year wait is a bureaucratic void. Design emotional, informational, and community support into this period to prepare parents for the real journey ahead.

Extend post-adoption support to 36 months

The 5% dissolution rate signals critical needs in years 2 and 3. Extend trained counselling beyond the current 12-month window to where families actually need it most.

Researcher's Reflection

What building these tools taught us about research itself

On accessing the subconscious

The DBT tool worked because it gave participants a role, advisor, rather than a position, subject. When people stop narrating and start reacting, the truth surfaces. This became a foundational principle for all sensitive research conversations we design.

On designing around stigma

Adoption is taboo in India in ways that direct research methods cannot penetrate. Every tool we built needed to create indirect access to the real question. The game, the map, the advisory frame: all were deliberate workarounds for a topic that closes the moment it is directly examined.

On the tool as an intervention

Several participants said the MYOS session was the first time they had articulated parts of their adoption journey, even to themselves. The research instrument became, quietly, a therapeutic one. This is the responsibility that comes with social design research.
